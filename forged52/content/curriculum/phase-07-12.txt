# FORGED52 curriculum, weeks 25-52. DRAFT for pastoral review.

## WEEK 25 | The Real Battle
foundation: Ephesians 6:10-20
truth: Our struggle is spiritual, and God gives us his armor to stand firm.
devotional: Paul reminds us that we do not wrestle against flesh and blood. The battle behind your marriage tension, your temptation and your discouragement is spiritual. But he doesn't say "try harder." He says "be strong in the Lord and in the strength of his might" and put on God's armor: truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation and the word of God, all held together by prayer. The armor is Christ himself.
pointman: Stop fighting your wife, your kids or your coworkers as if they're the enemy. Name the real enemy and put on the armor.
reflect: Where have you treated people as the enemy? || Which piece of armor do you neglect most? || How does prayer fit into spiritual warfare?
mission: Each morning this week, pray through the armor of God (Ephesians 6:14-18), applying each piece to your day.
discuss: How do Christians hold a biblical view of spiritual warfare without fear or superstition? || What does "standing firm" look like? || How can we stand together as brothers?
endweek: Where did you stand firm this week?
prayer: Lord, make me strong in your strength. Clothe me with your armor. Help me see the real battle and fight it with truth, faith, your word and prayer. Amen.

## WEEK 26 | Courage
foundation: Joshua 1:1-9
truth: Courage comes from God's presence, not our own confidence.
devotional: Moses is dead. Joshua has to lead a nation into hostile territory. Three times God says, "Be strong and courageous." But notice the reason: "for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go." Courage isn't the absence of fear; it's obedience while afraid because God is present. And God ties courage to his word: meditate on it day and night. You're halfway through the year. This is a week to recommit.
pointman: Halfway. You've read about half the Bible. Don't coast now. Be strong and courageous.
reflect: What are you afraid of right now? || Where is God asking you to act with courage? || How has reading Scripture this year shaped your confidence in God?
mission: Do one thing this week that requires courage: a hard conversation, a step of faith or standing up for what's right.
discuss: What does biblical courage look like in daily life? || When have you seen a man act courageously? || How do we encourage courage without bravado?
endweek: Where did you choose courage over comfort?
prayer: Lord, you are with me wherever I go. Make me strong and courageous. Let your word fill my mind so that fear doesn't rule my choices. Amen.
memory: JOS|1|9|9

## WEEK 27 | Resisting Temptation
foundation: Matthew 4:1-11; James 4:7-8
truth: Jesus overcame temptation with Scripture, and he gives us his strength to resist.
devotional: Led by the Spirit into the wilderness, hungry and alone, Jesus was tempted three ways: comfort (turn stones to bread), spectacle (throw yourself down) and power (worship me and have it all). Each time he answered with Scripture. Hebrews says he was tempted in every way as we are, yet without sin, so he can sympathize with our weakness. James gives the pattern: submit to God, resist the devil, and he will flee.
pointman: Arm yourself with specific verses for your specific temptations. You fight better with a sword you know how to use.
reflect: Which of Jesus' three temptations do you face most? || What verse could you use against your main temptation? || How does knowing Jesus sympathizes with you change your approach?
mission: Choose a verse that speaks directly to your biggest temptation. Memorize it and say it out loud when you're tempted.
discuss: Why did Jesus use Scripture rather than argument? || What does "draw near to God" look like in a moment of temptation? || How can we help each other resist?
endweek: When did you resist temptation this week, and what helped?
prayer: Jesus, you were tempted and did not sin. Help me resist with your word and your strength. When I fall, bring me back quickly to you. Amen.

## WEEK 28 | Perseverance
foundation: Hebrews 12:1-13; Galatians 6:9
truth: We run with endurance by fixing our eyes on Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith.
devotional: Hebrews 12 pictures a race with a crowd of faithful witnesses who have gone before. Lay aside every weight and sin, and run with endurance, looking to Jesus, who endured the cross for the joy set before him. Hardship isn't proof God has left you. It's often his loving discipline, training you for righteousness. Galatians adds: don't grow weary in doing good, for in due season you will reap if you don't give up.
pointman: Missed some days? You're not disqualified. Get back on the track. Grace runs with you.
reflect: What weight is slowing you down? || Where do you feel tempted to give up? || How does looking to Jesus help you endure?
mission: Identify one area where you've grown weary in doing good. Take one small step back into it this week.
discuss: What helps you keep going when motivation fades? || How does God use hardship to train us? || How can we help a brother who is ready to quit?
endweek: What helped you persevere this week?
prayer: Jesus, you endured the cross for the joy set before you. Help me run with endurance. Strip away what slows me down, and keep my eyes fixed on you. Amen.

## WEEK 29 | Humility
foundation: Philippians 2:1-11
truth: Jesus, though equal with God, humbled himself, and calls us to have the same mind.
devotional: Philippians 2 shows the greatest descent in history: Christ, in the form of God, emptied himself, took the form of a servant and became obedient to death on a cross. Humility isn't thinking less of yourself; it's thinking of yourself less and counting others more significant. Pride is the root of a lot of male sin: defensiveness, anger, refusing help, needing to be right. Humility is strength under God's control.
pointman: Humble men ask for help, admit when they're wrong and celebrate other men's wins. Practice all three this week.
reflect: Where does pride show up in your life most? || Who do you find it hard to count as more significant? || What would it look like to have the mind of Christ at home?
mission: Ask someone for help this week with something you'd normally handle alone, and publicly praise another man's work.
discuss: Why is pride so hard to see in ourselves? || How did Jesus model humility? || What is the difference between humility and low self-worth?
endweek: Where did God humble you this week?
prayer: Jesus, you humbled yourself for me. Root out my pride. Give me your mind, so that I count others more significant than myself and serve gladly. Amen.

## WEEK 30 | Forgiveness
foundation: Matthew 18:21-35; Colossians 3:12-14
truth: We forgive because we have been forgiven an unpayable debt.
devotional: Peter asks how many times he has to forgive, and Jesus answers with a story about a servant forgiven an impossible debt who then chokes a fellow servant over a small one. The point lands hard: unforgiveness doesn't fit people who have been forgiven by God. Forgiveness doesn't mean pretending it didn't hurt, removing all consequences or immediately restoring trust. It means releasing the debt to God and refusing revenge.
pointman: Bitterness is a poison men drink for years. Name the person. Bring it to God. Start the process of forgiving.
reflect: Who do you need to forgive? || What do you think forgiveness requires, and what doesn't it require? || How big is the debt God forgave you?
mission: Write down the name of someone you need to forgive and pray for them every day this week. If appropriate, reach out.
discuss: Why is forgiveness so hard for men? || What is the difference between forgiveness and reconciliation? || How do we forgive when the other person isn't sorry?
endweek: Where are you in the process of forgiving?
prayer: Father, you forgave me a debt I could never pay. Help me forgive as I've been forgiven. Heal the wounds I've carried and free me from bitterness. Amen.

## WEEK 31 | Compassion
foundation: Luke 10:25-37; Micah 6:8
truth: Loving your neighbor means stopping for the person in front of you.
devotional: A lawyer asks Jesus, "Who is my neighbor?" hoping to limit his responsibility. Jesus tells about a Samaritan, a despised outsider, who stops, bandages, carries and pays for a beaten stranger while religious leaders walk past. The question shifts from "Who deserves my help?" to "Who can I be a neighbor to?" Micah sums up what God requires: do justice, love kindness, walk humbly with your God.
pointman: Compassion is inconvenient by nature. Watch for the person God puts in your path this week, and stop.
reflect: Who are you tempted to walk past? || What keeps you from stopping for people in need? || How do justice and mercy work together?
mission: Help someone this week who can't repay you: a stranger, a widow, a struggling neighbor or a coworker in a hard season.
discuss: Who were the "Samaritans" of Jesus' day, and who are they today? || How can busy men practice compassion? || What does doing justice look like in your community?
endweek: Who did you stop for this week?
prayer: Lord, give me eyes to see the people I usually pass by. Make me a neighbor who stops, helps and loves without counting the cost. Amen.

## WEEK 32 | Serving Others
foundation: John 13:1-17; Mark 10:42-45
truth: The Son of Man came not to be served but to serve and to give his life as a ransom for many.
devotional: On his last night, Jesus took off his outer garment, wrapped a towel around his waist and washed his disciples' feet, including Judas's. Then he said, "I have given you an example." In Mark 10, he flips the world's view of greatness: whoever would be great must be a servant. Real authority in God's kingdom kneels. Service is not a phase of discipleship; it's the shape of it.
pointman: Find the towel in your life: the thankless task, the job nobody wants. Pick it up this week.
reflect: Where do you expect to be served? || What is the "towel" job in your home, church or work? || How does Jesus' service of Judas challenge you?
mission: Volunteer for the least glamorous job you can find this week at home, church or work.
discuss: Why did Jesus wash Judas's feet too? || How does serving change a man's heart? || What does servant leadership look like in a family?
endweek: What towel did you pick up this week?
prayer: Jesus, you washed feet and gave your life. Make me a servant like you. Let me find joy in serving, not in being served. Amen.

## WEEK 33 | Iron Sharpens Iron
foundation: Proverbs 27:17; Ecclesiastes 4:9-12
truth: God designed men to grow alongside brothers who sharpen, help and strengthen them.
devotional: "Iron sharpens iron, and one man sharpens another." Sharpening involves friction; real friendship isn't always comfortable. Ecclesiastes adds that two are better than one: if one falls, the other lifts him up, and a threefold cord is not quickly broken. Many men have plenty of acquaintances and no real friends. Isolation is dangerous. The enemy hunts the man who walks alone.
pointman: Who in your life can call you out and lift you up? If the answer is nobody, start a crew this week.
reflect: Who sharpens you right now? || What keeps you from deep friendships with other men? || Who could you reach out to this week?
mission: Invite one man to coffee or breakfast this week with the goal of going deeper than small talk.
discuss: Why do men struggle to build real friendships? || What makes a friendship sharpening rather than just fun? || How can this crew become a threefold cord?
endweek: Who sharpened you this week, and whom did you sharpen?
prayer: Lord, thank you for brothers. Give me friends who sharpen me, and make me a friend who strengthens others. Keep me from walking alone. Amen.

## WEEK 34 | Accountability
foundation: Galatians 6:1-5; James 5:16

devotional: Paul tells spiritual brothers to restore a man caught in sin "in a spirit of gentleness," watching themselves. James says, "Confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, that you may be healed." Accountability isn't a police force; it's a hospital. ## WEEK 36 | Unity
foundation: John 17:20-23; Psalm 133
truth: Unity among believers shows the world that Jesus was sent by the Father.
devotional: On the night before the cross, Jesus prayed for us: "that they may all be one... so that the world may believe that you have sent me." Christian unity isn't uniformity. It's oneness in Christ that holds across personality, politics and preference. Psalm 133 says unity is good and pleasant, like oil running down Aaron's beard. Division is easy. Unity takes humility, forgiveness and a shared focus on Jesus.

## WEEK 37 | Wisdom
foundation: James 1:5-8; Proverbs 2:1-6
truth: God gives wisdom generously to those who ask in faith and seek it like treasure.
devotional: James makes a remarkable promise: "If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God, who gives generously to all without reproach." Proverbs says to seek wisdom like silver and hidden treasure; then you'll understand the fear of the LORD. Wisdom isn't just knowing facts. It's skill in living rightly before God. Leaders need it more than talent. The good news: it's available for the asking.
pointman: Before your next big decision, pray, open Scripture and ask a wise counselor. In that order.
reflect: What decision do you need wisdom for right now? || Where do you go first for advice? || What does "the fear of the LORD" mean to you?

## WEEK 38 | Integrity
foundation: Psalm 15; Proverbs 10:9
truth: The man of integrity walks securely; the one who takes crooked paths will be found out.
devotional: Psalm 15 asks who may dwell with God and answers with a portrait of integrity: walks blamelessly, speaks truth in his heart, doesn't slander, keeps his word even when it hurts. Proverbs adds that whoever walks in integrity walks securely. Integrity means being one thing all the way through. A leader's influence rests on it. It's built in small promises kept and lost in small compromises justified.

## WEEK 41 | The Gospel
foundation: 1 Corinthians 15:1-8; Romans 1:16-17
truth: The gospel is the good news that Christ died for our sins, was buried and was raised, and it is God's power for salvation.
devotional: Paul passes on what is "of first importance": Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, he was buried, he was raised on the third day, and he appeared to many witnesses. That's the core. In Romans he says he's not ashamed of it, because it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes. Before you can share the gospel, you need to be able to say it clearly and simply.
pointman: Practice saying the gospel in under two minutes. If you can't explain it, you can't share it.

endweek: Did you get a chance to share the gospel this week?

## WEEK 42 | Your Testimony

## WEEK 43 | Living the Gospel

truth: Followers of Jesus are salt and light whose good works point people to the Father.
devotional: Jesus says, "You are the salt of the earth... You are the light of the world." Not "try to be." Salt preserves and flavors; light exposes and guides. Neither does any good hidden away. Integrity, kindness and joy make the gospel believable.

discuss: How can Christians be salt without being preachy? || When has someone's life drawn you toward God? || What dims our light?
endweek: Where did your light shine this week?
prayer: Lord, make me salt and light. Let my good works point people to you, not to me. Let my life make the gospel attractive. Amen.

## WEEK 49 | The Hope of Glory
foundation: Revelation 21:1-7
truth: God will make all things new and dwell with his people, wiping away every tear.
devotional: The Bible begins in a garden and ends in a city. John sees a new heaven and a new earth, and a loud voice says, "Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man."
