B|ISA|Isaiah son of Amoz|Jerusalem, about 740-680 BC, as Assyria threatened|God is the Holy One who judges sin, yet promises comfort, a suffering Servant and a new creation.|Isaiah is quoted all over the New Testament: the virgin birth (7:14), the child called Mighty God (9:6), the Servant pierced for our transgressions (53).
B|JER|Jeremiah, with his scribe Baruch|Judah's last 40 years, about 627-580 BC|The "weeping prophet" warns Judah for decades, watches Jerusalem fall, and announces a new covenant written on hearts.|Jesus inaugurates the new covenant of Jeremiah 31 in his blood (Luke 22:20).
B|LAM|Traditionally Jeremiah|Just after Jerusalem's destruction in 586 BC|Five poems of grief over Jerusalem's fall, with hope in the middle: God's mercies are new every morning.|Jesus wept over Jerusalem and bore God's judgment so his people could be restored.
B|EZK|Ezekiel, a priest in exile|Babylon, about 593-571 BC|God's glory leaves the corrupt temple, judgment falls, but God promises new hearts, dry bones raised and his glory returning.|Jesus is the Good Shepherd Ezekiel promised (34) and the one who brings new hearts by the Spirit.
B|DAN|Daniel|Babylon and Persia, about 605-530 BC|Faithfulness in a pagan culture, and visions showing God rules history and will give an everlasting kingdom to "one like a son of man."|Jesus called himself the Son of Man from Daniel 7 more than any other title.
