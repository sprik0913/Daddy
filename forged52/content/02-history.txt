B|JOS|Traditionally Joshua, with later additions|Canaan, around 1400-1370 BC|God keeps his promise and gives Israel the land as they trust him, fight, and divide it among the tribes.|Joshua's name is the Hebrew form of "Jesus" (the LORD saves); Jesus leads his people into the true rest (Hebrews 4).
B|JDG|Unknown, possibly Samuel|Israel before the kings, about 1370-1050 BC|A downward spiral: Israel turns to idols, gets oppressed, cries out, God sends a judge to rescue, and they fall again. "Everyone did what was right in his own eyes."|Every flawed judge shows the need for a perfect rescuer and king.
B|RUT|Unknown|During the time of the judges|A loyal Moabite widow and a kind Israelite redeemer show God's quiet faithfulness, leading to King David.|Boaz the kinsman-redeemer pictures Jesus, and Ruth is in Jesus' family line (Matthew 1:5).
B|1SA|Unknown, using records from Samuel, Nathan and Gad|Israel's shift from judges to kings, about 1100-1010 BC|Israel asks for a king like the nations. Saul starts strong and fails; God chooses David, a man after his own heart.|David, the shepherd king from Bethlehem, points to Jesus, the Son of David.
B|2SA|Unknown|David's reign, about 1010-970 BC|David's victories, God's covenant promising an eternal throne, then David's sin with Bathsheba and the family fallout.|God promises David a son whose kingdom will last forever (7:12-16), fulfilled in Jesus.
B|1KI|Unknown, compiled during the exile|Solomon to Ahab, about 970-850 BC|Solomon's wisdom and temple, then his heart drifts, the kingdom splits, and prophets like Elijah confront idolatrous kings.|Jesus is greater than Solomon (Matthew 12:42) and the true temple.
B|2KI|Unknown, compiled during the exile|Elisha to the fall of Jerusalem, about 850-586 BC|Both kingdoms keep sinning despite the prophets; Assyria takes Israel and Babylon takes Judah.|Elisha's miracles foreshadow Jesus', and the surviving king at the end keeps David's line alive.
B|1CH|Traditionally Ezra|Written after the exile, retelling history from Adam to David|For returned exiles: you are still God's people. Genealogies, David's reign and preparation for worship.|The focus on David's line and the temple points to Jesus, the Son of David.
B|2CH|Traditionally Ezra|Written after the exile; Solomon to the exile|Judah's story told through its kings and temple worship: seeking God brings blessing, forsaking him brings ruin.|The book ends with permission to rebuild, a hope fully met in Jesus.
B|EZR|Ezra|Return from Babylon, 538-450s BC|God moves a pagan king to send exiles home; they rebuild the temple, and Ezra calls them back to God's word.|God keeping his people and his promises after exile sets the stage for Jesus.
B|NEH|Nehemiah|Jerusalem, around 445-430 BC|Nehemiah rebuilds Jerusalem's walls in 52 days despite opposition, and the people recommit to God's law.|Nehemiah's leadership and prayer show the kind of godly leader fulfilled in Jesus.
B|EST|Unknown|Persia, around 480 BC|God is never named, yet he is clearly at work: Esther risks her life and the Jews are saved from genocide.|Esther stands in the gap for her people at great risk, a shadow of Jesus' far greater rescue.
