# Hard questions. Same format as manhood.txt. "views:" lists where Christians differ (|| separated).

## suffering | Why does God allow suffering?
refs: GEN.50.20 ; ROM.8.18-28 ; REV.21.3-5 ; JOB.38.1-7 ; JHN.11.33-35
summary: Scripture does not give one tidy answer, but it gives a God who suffers with us and promises to end suffering.

The Bible takes suffering seriously. It traces much of it to the fall: a world broken by sin, both our own and others’. It shows that God can bring good even out of evil done to us, as Joseph told his brothers. It promises a day when God will wipe away every tear.

The book of Job warns against easy explanations. God never tells Job why he suffered; he reveals himself. At the cross, God himself enters suffering. Jesus wept at Lazarus’ tomb even though he was about to raise him.

views: Christians emphasize different aspects: God’s sovereignty over all things (Reformed), the reality of human free will (Arminian/Wesleyan) and the mystery of evil (many traditions). All affirm God is good and evil is real.

## exclusive | Is Jesus the only way to God?
refs: JHN.14.6 ; ACT.4.12 ; 1TI.2.5-6 ; ROM.10.9-15
summary: Jesus claimed to be the only way to the Father, and the apostles proclaimed salvation in his name alone.

Jesus said, "I am the way, the truth and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me." Peter declared there is no other name under heaven by which we must be saved. This can sound narrow, but the invitation is wide: whoever calls on the name of the Lord will be saved.

Christianity is unique in that it says salvation is a gift, not an achievement. That is why it centers on a person, not a program.

views: Christians agree salvation is through Christ alone. They differ on questions like the fate of those who never heard the gospel, and many leave that with a just and merciful God.

## reliable | Can I trust the Bible?
refs: 2TI.3.16-17 ; 2PE.1.16-21 ; LUK.1.1-4 ; PSA.19.7-11
summary: The Bible claims to be God-breathed, and it has strong historical and manuscript support.

Paul writes that all Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching and training. Peter says the apostles were eyewitnesses, not followers of clever myths. Luke says he carefully investigated everything.

Historically, the New Testament is attested by thousands of early manuscripts, far more than any other ancient work. Archaeology has confirmed many people and places it names. Differences among manuscripts are mostly minor and do not affect core doctrine.

views: Christians hold differing views on how to describe inspiration (for example, inerrancy versus infallibility), and Protestants, Catholics and Orthodox recognize different numbers of Old Testament books.

## unanswered | Why doesn’t God answer my prayers?
refs: MAT.7.7-11 ; 2CO.12.7-10 ; JAS.4.2-3 ; LUK.22.42
summary: God hears every prayer. Sometimes the answer is yes, sometimes no, sometimes wait.

Jesus encourages persistent prayer and promises a good Father. Yet Paul asked three times for his thorn to be removed and God said, "My grace is sufficient for you." Jesus himself prayed "not my will but yours."

James also notes we sometimes ask with wrong motives. Silence can be a season of growth. Keep praying, keep a record and remember that God’s goal is to make us more like Christ.

## doubt | Is it wrong to doubt?
refs: MRK.9.24 ; JUD.1.22 ; JHN.20.24-29 ; PSA.73.1-28
summary: Doubt is not the opposite of faith. Unbelief that refuses to seek is.

A desperate father cried, "I believe; help my unbelief!" and Jesus healed his son. Jude tells believers to have mercy on those who doubt. Thomas doubted and Jesus met him with evidence.

Bring your doubts to God and to mature believers. Study, ask questions and keep obeying what you do know.

## hell | What does the Bible say about hell?
refs: MAT.25.31-46 ; 2TH.1.7-10 ; REV.20.11-15 ; 2PE.3.9
summary: Jesus spoke of final judgment more than anyone else, and God desires that none perish.

Jesus warned of judgment, of outer darkness and of eternal punishment. Scripture presents judgment as just, taking evil seriously. It also says God is patient, not wanting any to perish but all to reach repentance.

views: Most Christians historically have held to conscious eternal punishment. Some evangelicals hold to conditional immortality (annihilation). All affirm a real final judgment and urge people to Christ.

## science | Do faith and science conflict?
refs: PSA.19.1-4 ; ROM.1.20 ; GEN.1.1 ; COL.1.16-17
summary: The heavens declare the glory of God. Many founders of modern science were Christians.

Scripture says creation reveals God’s power. Christians believe God made an orderly world that can be studied. Science answers how things work; Scripture answers who made them and why.

views: Christians hold different views on the age of the earth and how to read Genesis 1 (young-earth, old-earth, framework and evolutionary creation). All affirm God as Creator.

## sin-saved | Can a Christian keep sinning?
refs: ROM.6.1-14 ; 1JN.1.8-10 ; 1JN.2.1-2 ; 1JN.3.6-9 ; GAL.5.16-17
summary: Christians still sin, but they cannot be at peace with sin.

John says if we say we have no sin we deceive ourselves, and also that whoever is born of God does not keep on sinning. Paul asks, "Shall we sin so grace may abound? By no means!" The Christian life involves real struggle between flesh and Spirit, real confession and real growth.

## assurance | Can I know I’m saved? Can salvation be lost?
refs: 1JN.5.11-13 ; JHN.10.27-30 ; ROM.8.1 ; ROM.8.38-39 ; EPH.2.8-9
summary: John wrote so that believers may know they have eternal life.

Assurance rests on Christ’s finished work, not your performance. There is no condemnation for those in Christ. Fruit in your life, love for God and others, and the Spirit’s witness confirm genuine faith.

views: Christians differ on whether a true believer can lose salvation (Reformed "perseverance of the saints" versus Arminian views). All affirm salvation is by grace through faith.

## divorce | What does the Bible say about divorce?
refs: MAT.19.3-9 ; MAL.2.13-16 ; 1CO.7.10-16
summary: God designed marriage for life, and Scripture takes divorce seriously while offering grace.

Jesus pointed back to creation: what God has joined together, let no one separate. Scripture names some circumstances (for example, sexual immorality and abandonment) in which divorce is permitted. Abuse requires immediate safety and pastoral help.

views: Churches differ on permitted grounds and remarriage. If you face this, talk with your pastor. God’s grace extends to the divorced.

## anxiety | Is anxiety a lack of faith?
refs: PHP.4.6-7 ; MAT.6.25-34 ; 1PE.5.7 ; PSA.42.1-11
summary: Scripture invites us to bring anxiety to God, and it does not shame those who struggle.

Paul says do not be anxious about anything, but in everything by prayer present your requests to God. The psalmist asks, "Why are you cast down, O my soul?" Godly people struggled with fear and despair.

Anxiety and depression can have physical causes. Seeking counseling or medical care is wise, not faithless. If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (US) or local emergency services.

## gifts | What is my spiritual gift?
refs: 1CO.12.4-11 ; ROM.12.4-8 ; 1PE.4.10-11 ; EPH.4.11-13
summary: Every believer has gifts from the Spirit to serve the church.

Paul lists gifts such as teaching, serving, encouraging, giving, leading and mercy. The point is not to label yourself, but to serve. Gifts are often discovered by serving and listening to others’ feedback.

views: Christians differ on whether certain miraculous gifts (tongues, prophecy) continue today (continuationist versus cessationist views).

## pharaoh | Why did God harden Pharaoh’s heart?
refs: EXO.4.21 ; EXO.8.15 ; EXO.8.32 ; EXO.9.12 ; ROM.9.17-18
summary: Exodus says both that Pharaoh hardened his own heart and that God hardened it.

Exodus describes Pharaoh hardening his own heart several times (for example 8:15 and 8:32) and also says the Lord hardened his heart (9:12 and later). The text holds both together: Pharaoh freely resisted God, and God confirmed and used that resistance to display his power and rescue Israel. Paul quotes this in Romans 9 to show God’s sovereign freedom in mercy and judgment.

Application: persistent resistance to God is dangerous. Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your heart (Hebrews 3:15).

views: Reformed readers emphasize God’s sovereign decree; Arminian and Wesleyan readers emphasize that God gave Pharaoh over to a hardness he chose. Both affirm Pharaoh was responsible and God is just.

## death | What happens when we die?
refs: 2CO.5.6-8 ; PHP.1.21-23 ; LUK.23.42-43 ; 1TH.4.13-18 ; 1CO.15.51-55
summary: For the believer, to be absent from the body is to be present with the Lord, awaiting the resurrection.

Jesus told the thief on the cross, "Today you will be with me in Paradise." Paul says that departing to be with Christ is far better. Scripture also looks beyond that to the resurrection of the body when Christ returns, when death itself is swallowed up in victory.

The Christian hope is not only going to heaven when we die, but bodily resurrection and life in a renewed creation.

views: Most Christians believe in a conscious "intermediate state" with Christ before the resurrection. Some hold to "soul sleep." Catholic teaching includes purgatory, which Protestants reject.

## salvation | What is salvation?
refs: EPH.2.1-10 ; ROM.3.23-26 ; ROM.10.9-10 ; TIT.3.4-7 ; JHN.3.16-18
summary: Salvation is God rescuing sinners from sin and death through Jesus Christ, received by faith.

All have sinned and fall short of God’s glory. Jesus died for our sins and rose again. Salvation is by grace through faith, not by works, so no one can boast. Yet we are saved for good works, which flow from new life rather than earning it.

Salvation includes forgiveness (justification), a new heart and growth in holiness (sanctification) and the future hope of glory.

views: Protestants emphasize justification by faith alone. Catholic and Orthodox traditions describe salvation more as a process involving the sacraments. All affirm that salvation is God’s gracious work in Christ.

## judgment | If God forgives, why is there a final judgment?
refs: 2CO.5.10 ; ROM.8.1 ; REV.20.11-15 ; ACT.17.30-31 ; JHN.5.24
summary: Judgment shows that God takes evil seriously and sets the world right. In Christ, believers face it without condemnation.

A good judge does not ignore evil. The final judgment means that every injustice will be answered. For those in Christ, there is no condemnation, because Jesus bore judgment for them. Jesus said whoever believes has passed from death to life.

Believers will still give an account of their lives (2 Corinthians 5:10), not to determine salvation but to receive what God in grace rewards.

## ot-hard | Why are there difficult passages in the Old Testament?
refs: DEU.20.16-18 ; GEN.15.16 ; EZK.18.23 ; MAT.19.8 ; HEB.1.1-2
summary: Some passages are hard. Read them in context, in the whole story of Scripture, and in light of Jesus.

Passages about the conquest of Canaan, harsh laws or violence are genuinely difficult. Context helps: God waited centuries before judging Canaan (Genesis 15:16), these commands were limited to a specific time, and God takes no pleasure in the death of the wicked (Ezekiel 18:23). Jesus taught that some laws were concessions to hardened hearts. Describing an event is not the same as approving it.

God’s revelation unfolds over time and reaches its fullness in Jesus. It is fine to wrestle; many faithful believers have.

views: Christians explain these texts differently (for example, hyperbolic ancient war language, unique judgment, progressive revelation). Honest humility is appropriate.

## warfare | What is spiritual warfare?
refs: EPH.6.10-18 ; 1PE.5.8-9 ; JAS.4.7 ; 2CO.10.3-5 ; COL.2.15
summary: Christians face real spiritual opposition, and Christ has already won the decisive victory.

Paul says our struggle is not against flesh and blood but against spiritual forces of evil, and he describes the armor of God: truth, righteousness, the gospel, faith, salvation, the Word and prayer. Peter warns that the devil prowls like a lion. Yet Christ disarmed the powers at the cross.

Spiritual warfare is mostly ordinary faithfulness: knowing Scripture, praying, resisting temptation, confessing sin and standing together.

## revelation | What does the book of Revelation teach?
refs: REV.1.1-3 ; REV.5.9-10 ; REV.19.11-16 ; REV.21.1-5 ; REV.22.20
summary: Revelation is a vision of Jesus reigning, his people persevering and God making all things new.

Revelation was written to suffering churches. It uses symbolic imagery (much drawn from Daniel, Ezekiel and Zechariah) to show that the Lamb who was slain is on the throne, that evil will be judged and that God will dwell with his people in a new creation.

Its main call is faithful endurance and worship. It ends with a prayer: "Come, Lord Jesus."

views: Interpreters read it as mostly future (futurist), fulfilled in the first century (preterist), describing church history (historicist) or as timeless symbols of the battle between good and evil (idealist). Many combine these.

## endtimes | How do Christians understand end-times prophecy?
refs: MAT.24.36-44 ; ACT.1.6-11 ; 1TH.5.1-11 ; 2PE.3.8-13 ; REV.20.1-6
summary: Christians agree Jesus will return bodily, judge the world and renew creation. They differ on the details.

Jesus said no one knows the day or hour, and he called his followers to stay awake and be ready. The angels promised he would return the same way he left. Peter says the delay is God’s patience.

views: Premillennial views (including dispensational and historic premillennialism) expect Christ to return before a thousand-year reign. Amillennial views read the thousand years as the present reign of Christ. Postmillennial views expect the gospel to bring a long era of flourishing before Christ returns. Christians also differ on a rapture. All affirm the blessed hope of his return.

## heaven | What does the Bible teach about heaven and the new earth?
refs: REV.21.1-4 ; REV.22.1-5 ; ISA.65.17-25 ; ROM.8.19-23 ; 1CO.2.9
summary: The final hope is not floating in clouds but God dwelling with his people in a renewed heaven and earth.

Scripture ends with a new heaven and new earth, a holy city, no more death or mourning and God’s people seeing his face. Creation itself will be set free from decay. The best thing about heaven is God himself.

This hope gives meaning to present faithfulness: nothing done in the Lord is wasted (1 Corinthians 15:58).
