B|GEN|Traditionally Moses|Written for Israel in the wilderness, around 1400s BC; covers creation to about 1800 BC|God creates a good world, people break it with sin, and God starts his rescue plan through one family: Abraham, Isaac, Jacob and Joseph.|God promises a descendant of Eve who will crush the serpent (3:15) and a blessing for all nations through Abraham's offspring. Jesus is that offspring.
B|EXO|Traditionally Moses|Egypt and the Sinai desert, around 1400s BC|God hears his people's cry, breaks Egypt's grip, brings them out, gives them his law and comes to live among them in the tabernacle.|The Passover lamb whose blood saves points to Jesus, "our Passover lamb" (1 Cor 5:7). The tabernacle shows God dwelling with people, which Jesus fulfills (John 1:14).
B|LEV|Traditionally Moses|At Mount Sinai, around 1400s BC|A holy God makes a way for sinful people to live near him through sacrifices, priests and a set-apart way of life.|Every sacrifice and the Day of Atonement point to Jesus, the final high priest and the once-for-all sacrifice (Hebrews 9-10).
B|NUM|Traditionally Moses|The 40 years in the wilderness between Sinai and Canaan|Israel complains and rebels, and a whole generation dies in the desert, but God stays faithful and brings their children to the edge of the promised land.|Jesus compares himself to the bronze snake lifted up so people could look and live (John 3:14-15).
B|DEU|Traditionally Moses|On the plains of Moab, just before Israel enters Canaan|Moses' farewell sermons: remember what God did, love him with everything, obey his word, and choose life.|Jesus quotes Deuteronomy to defeat Satan in the wilderness, and he is the prophet like Moses that God promised (18:15).
GEN 1|God creates the heavens, the earth and everything in them in six days, makes people in his image, and calls it all very good.
GEN 2|God rests on the seventh day, forms Adam from dust, plants Eden, gives one command, and creates Eve as Adam's partner.
GEN 3|The serpent deceives Eve, Adam and Eve eat the forbidden fruit, sin and death enter, and God promises a coming victory over the serpent.
GEN 4|Cain murders his brother Abel out of jealousy, sin spreads through Cain's line, and Seth is born as a new hope.
GEN 5|The family line from Adam to Noah, with the refrain "and he died," broken by Enoch, who walked with God.
GEN 6|Humanity becomes violent and corrupt, God grieves and decides to send a flood, but Noah finds favor and builds an ark.
GEN 7|Noah, his family and the animals enter the ark, and the flood covers the earth for months.
GEN 8|God remembers Noah, the waters recede, Noah sends out birds, leaves the ark and offers a sacrifice.
GEN 9|God blesses Noah, makes a covenant never to flood the earth again with the rainbow as its sign, and Noah's drunkenness exposes his sons.
GEN 10|The table of nations: how Noah's sons Shem, Ham and Japheth spread out and became the peoples of the world.
GEN 11|People build the tower of Babel to make a name for themselves, God scatters them, and the line of Shem leads to Abram.
GEN 12|God calls Abram to leave home and promises land, offspring and blessing for all nations; Abram lies about Sarai in Egypt.
GEN 13|Abram and Lot separate, Lot chooses the land near Sodom, and God repeats his promise of land to Abram.
GEN 14|Abram rescues Lot from invading kings and is blessed by Melchizedek, priest-king of Salem.
GEN 15|God promises Abram descendants like the stars, Abram believes and it is counted as righteousness, and God seals a covenant.
GEN 16|Sarai gives Hagar to Abram, Hagar runs away pregnant, and God meets her as "the God who sees me"; Ishmael is born.
GEN 17|God renames Abram and Sarai as Abraham and Sarah, gives circumcision as the covenant sign, and promises Isaac.
GEN 18|Three visitors promise Sarah a son and she laughs; Abraham pleads with God to spare Sodom for the sake of the righteous.
GEN 19|Angels rescue Lot's family, Sodom and Gomorrah are destroyed, Lot's wife looks back, and Lot's daughters commit incest.
GEN 20|Abraham again passes Sarah off as his sister, this time to Abimelech, and God protects her.
GEN 21|Isaac is born as promised, Hagar and Ishmael are sent away but God cares for them, and Abraham makes a treaty at Beersheba.
GEN 22|God tests Abraham by asking him to sacrifice Isaac, then stops him and provides a ram: "The LORD will provide."
GEN 23|Sarah dies, and Abraham buys the cave of Machpelah as a burial place, his first piece of the promised land.
GEN 24|Abraham's servant prays for guidance and finds Rebekah as a wife for Isaac.
GEN 25|Abraham dies, Rebekah gives birth to twins, and Esau sells his birthright to Jacob for a bowl of stew.
GEN 26|God renews the promise to Isaac, Isaac repeats his father's lie about his wife, and digs wells amid conflict.
GEN 27|Rebekah and Jacob deceive blind Isaac, and Jacob steals Esau's blessing; Esau vows to kill him.
GEN 28|Jacob flees, dreams of a stairway to heaven at Bethel, and God promises to be with him.
GEN 29|Jacob works seven years for Rachel, is tricked into marrying Leah, and works seven more; Leah begins having sons.
GEN 30|Rivalry between Leah and Rachel produces more sons, Rachel finally bears Joseph, and Jacob prospers with Laban's flocks.
GEN 31|Jacob secretly leaves Laban, Rachel steals the household gods, and Jacob and Laban make a peace covenant.
GEN 32|Jacob prepares to meet Esau in fear, wrestles with God through the night, and is renamed Israel.
GEN 33|Esau runs to embrace Jacob, the brothers reconcile, and Jacob settles near Shechem.
GEN 34|Dinah is violated by Shechem, and her brothers Simeon and Levi take brutal revenge on the city.
GEN 35|Jacob returns to Bethel and gets rid of foreign gods, God reaffirms his promise, Rachel dies giving birth to Benjamin, and Isaac dies.
GEN 36|The family line of Esau, the father of the Edomites.
GEN 37|Jacob favors Joseph, Joseph dreams of ruling his family, and his jealous brothers sell him into slavery.
GEN 38|Judah fails his daughter-in-law Tamar, she tricks him into fathering her twins, and Judah admits she was more righteous.
GEN 39|Joseph prospers in Potiphar's house, refuses the advances of Potiphar's wife, and is thrown in prison, but God is with him.
GEN 40|In prison Joseph interprets the dreams of Pharaoh's cupbearer and baker; the cupbearer forgets him.
GEN 41|Joseph interprets Pharaoh's dreams of seven good years and seven years of famine and is made second in command over Egypt.
GEN 42|Famine drives Joseph's brothers to Egypt; Joseph recognizes them, tests them and keeps Simeon.
GEN 43|The brothers return with Benjamin, and Joseph hosts them at a feast.
GEN 44|Joseph frames Benjamin with a silver cup, and Judah offers himself in Benjamin's place.
GEN 45|Joseph reveals himself and forgives his brothers: "It was not you who sent me here, but God."
GEN 46|Jacob takes his whole family to Egypt with God's promise to go with him, and is reunited with Joseph.
GEN 47|Jacob blesses Pharaoh, the family settles in Goshen, and Joseph manages Egypt through the famine.
GEN 48|Jacob blesses Joseph's sons, Ephraim and Manasseh, putting the younger ahead of the older.
GEN 49|Jacob blesses his twelve sons and promises that the scepter will not depart from Judah; Jacob dies.
GEN 50|Joseph buries Jacob, reassures his brothers that "you meant evil, but God meant it for good," and dies trusting God's promise.
